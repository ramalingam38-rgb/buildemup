VERBATIM EXCERPT:

25.1 General

25.1.1 Effective Length
The effective length of a column shall be calculated as per provisions
of Annex E.

25.1.2 Slenderness Limits for Columns
A column may be classified as short or long depending on its
slenderness ratio. A column is regarded as short if both the slenderness
ratios lex/D and ley/b are less than 12 (where lex = effective length
in respect of the major axis, D = depth in respect of the major axis,
ley = effective length in respect of the minor axis, b = width of the
member). It shall otherwise be considered as a slender column.

25.3 Slenderness Limits for Columns
The unsupported length between end restraints shall not exceed 60 times
the least lateral dimension of a column.

For a column where one end of the column is unrestrained, its
unsupported length shall not exceed 100 b²/D where b = width of that
cross-section, and D = depth of the cross-section measured in the
plane under consideration.

NOTES (BuildemUp interpretation):

For our preliminary residential design:
  - Floor height typically 3000mm
  - Effective length factor ~ 0.75 for braced columns (typical residential)
  - So effective length = 3000 * 0.75 = 2250mm
  - For 230mm column: ratio = 2250 / 230 = 9.78 → SHORT (no buckling check)
  - For 300mm column: ratio = 2250 / 300 = 7.5 → SHORT

Most residential columns are short. The slenderness check exists to
flag the rare case where a stilt parking column is unbraced over its
full height (no intermediate floor) — there it can become slender.
