# 02 — Screening Criteria + Vetting Questions

**Goal:** Filter the 8-12 longlist to 3-4 shortlist, then pick one. Avoid hiring someone too senior (wrong tool for an audit), too junior (won't catch nuance), or too commercial (wrong calibration for self-build single-dwelling-unit).

## HARD requirements (disqualifiers if missing)

| Requirement | Why | How to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Current COA registration | Required by Architects Act 1972; signals legal practising right | `coa.gov.in` search by name + state |
| 5+ years post-licensure practice | Below 5 years tends to be drafting/junior-only; can't critique systemic decisions | Ask directly; check LinkedIn/portfolio dates |
| Recent (last 24 months) residential/small-plot commission | Calibrates the reviewer to our use case (single-family 1000-3000 sqft plots) | Ask for 3 anonymized recent project descriptions |
| English written fluency | Review deliverable will be in English; back-and-forth must be smooth | Their initial reply tells you |
| Willingness to write up findings in a structured format | We need addressable bullet points, not a meandering memo | Ask explicitly; include sample question template |

## RED FLAGS (skip the candidate)

- **Pushes back on the audit-style engagement, prefers to "design something for you":** wrong fit. We don't need a designer, we need a reviewer.
- **Demands above ₹50K for this scope:** out of budget. Either negotiate or skip.
- **Refuses to write findings down, prefers to "talk through it":** unusable. We need a written deliverable.
- **No COA-active number:** non-starter, do not engage.
- **Tries to claim authorship/IP of the project after review:** misframed engagement; clarify or walk away. Project IP stays with Ramalingam.
- **Recommends ignoring NBC because "everyone does":** wrong epistemics; this is a soul-violation given the project's transparency goals.
- **Long pause (>14 days) between exchanges, no acknowledgment:** likely overcommitted; you'll miss the window.

## VETTING QUESTIONS (use after first reply, before signing)

Send these 7 questions in a single email. Most candidates answer in writing within 2-3 days. The ones who answer thoughtfully are the ones to engage.

1. **Calibration:** "Looking at a typical 1500-2400 sq ft urban Chennai residential plot with G+1 construction for a single family, what are the 3 most-commonly-violated TNCDBR rules you encounter in plan approval — in your own practice in the last 12 months?"
   - *What to look for:* Concrete examples. Specific rule numbers cited. No hedging.

2. **NBC 2016 currency:** "Are you aware of any NBC 2016 amendments or updates published between 2023 and 2026 that affect single-family residential design? If so, which?"
   - *What to look for:* Honest "I'm not aware" beats wrong-confident. Bonus points for naming a specific amendment.

3. **Critique experience:** "Have you ever reviewed someone else's architectural drawings or specifications for them — not as a client revision but as a peer critique? If so, briefly describe one."
   - *What to look for:* Concrete example with structured feedback. If they've only "reviewed for clients," ask follow-up: how do you structure feedback when the goal is internal correction, not external approval?

4. **Software audit comfort:** "We have a software system that emits architectural decisions (room sizes, setbacks, corridor widths, door placements, structural grid, drawings) for a given site. The review is of the system's logic, not a specific drawing. Are you comfortable critiquing the rules embedded in a spec, even when no physical site is involved?"
   - *What to look for:* Either yes-with-curiosity or "I'd need a concrete example to evaluate." Both are fine. A flat "I prefer drawings" is a misfit.

5. **Compensation expectation:** "For a 6-10 hour engagement to (a) review 5-7 spec documents totaling ~80 pages, (b) flag specific clauses you disagree with or want refined, and (c) deliver a 2-4 page written summary, what would you charge?"
   - *What to look for:* Anything from ₹15K to ₹40K is acceptable. Above ₹50K is out of scope. Below ₹10K is suspicious (low effort signal).

6. **Timeline:** "If we engaged you today, when could you realistically deliver the written summary?"
   - *What to look for:* Within 14-21 days is healthy. Beyond 30 days is too slow given calendar pressure. "Next week" is suspicious (probably skim review).

7. **Sample disagreement:** "Without seeing the spec yet — what's one architectural design choice in residential plan generation where you'd want to push back on a software-generated default? Example: corridor width, room aspect ratio, staircase placement, anything where you've seen software get it wrong."
   - *What to look for:* Substance. Specific opinions with reasoning. If they can't think of one, they probably won't push back on our defaults either.
