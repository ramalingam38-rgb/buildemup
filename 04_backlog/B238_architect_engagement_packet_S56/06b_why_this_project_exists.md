# Why This Project Exists

**A one-page note for the reviewing architect.**

---

The founder built his own home in Tamil Nadu a few years ago. Like most Indian homeowners doing this for the first time, he engaged a contractor who also brought along an architect and a structural engineer. The fees were quoted as a single bundled number. Materials were procured by the contractor. Labour was managed by the contractor. Compliance filings were handled by the contractor's office. Drawings were produced by the architect, who didn't speak to the homeowner directly after the first meeting. The structural engineer was named on the file but never visited the site.

The home was completed. The home stands. The home is fine.

But the founder could not — and still cannot — answer basic questions about it: What did the steel actually cost? What did the architect get paid? What was the labour share? What was the contractor's margin? Was the corridor width chosen because of NBC or because of the architect's habit? Why is the staircase placed where it is — was that mandated, optimized, or arbitrary?

The information was withheld. Not maliciously — there was no malice. The information was withheld because the industry's standard practice is to withhold it. The homeowner is presumed to be unable to handle the complexity. The professional class — architect, engineer, contractor — are presumed to handle it on the homeowner's behalf. The price of that presumption is a permanent loss of agency.

---

BuildEase exists to invert that presumption.

**Premise:** the homeowner, given the right interface, can absolutely handle the complexity. Not all of it. Not at expert depth. But enough of it to ask better questions, demand better justifications, and choose between trade-offs with clarity instead of trust-fall.

**Concrete claims the product makes to the homeowner:**

1. Here is the maximum house you can legally build on your plot, given your jurisdiction's rules. We'll show you the rules. We'll cite the clause.
2. Here is the house your budget actually supports. We'll show you the unit rates. We'll break out materials, labour, contractor margin, engineer fee separately.
3. Here are the rooms you said you wanted. Here is what fits. Here is what doesn't. Here is what we'd suggest you adjust, and why.
4. Here is your layout. Here are the alternatives we considered. Here is why we picked this one.
5. Here is your structural grid. Here is why columns go where they go. Here is the NBC clause / IS standard that says so.
6. Here are your drawings — one for the engineer who will build the house, one for the municipal authority who will approve it.
7. When a contractor sends you a quote, paste it here. We'll tell you where it deviates from canonical city rates, and whether the deviations have legitimate explanations or whether you should question them.

**That last item is the whole product, in seed form.** Everything else exists to make item 7 unbluffable.

---

This is where you come in.

The product's rules — every setback, every FAR calculation, every corridor minimum, every soil-class default, every severity classification on a compliance violation — must be DEFENSIBLE. Not "we made it up." Not "the developer's gut feeling." Not "the AI suggested it."

DEFENSIBLE means: a competent architect with current-day plan-approval exposure can look at each rule and say: "Yes, that's correct" — or "No, here's why that's wrong, and here's what it should be instead."

That defensibility cannot be self-certified by the founder, who isn't an architect. It cannot be certified by the software collaborator (Claude), which lacks current-day field exposure. It can only be certified by someone like you.

**Your written critique — every point you push back on, every default you flag as off-target, every framing you suggest reworking — is the missing legitimacy layer of the product.**

We don't need you to make it perfect. We need you to make it accountable to the same standards a competent architect would apply to their own work. After you, the founder + Claude do the rest: take each finding seriously, decide whether to act on it, file the disagreements, document the agreements.

---

Thank you for the engagement. Push back hard. Your dissent is the deliverable.
